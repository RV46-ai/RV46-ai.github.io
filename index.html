Operations with no Customer Success in it

Where the work had nothing to do with software or customers

Three engagements that needed operating systems built from nothing. None of them had a Customer Success function, and two had no software at all.

Food manufacturing, operations design

A production floor with no roles and no rhythm

No defined roles, no production cadence, no inventory control, and a workforce that could not be trained from English documents. I designed the operating system around that constraint: photo-based consumption boards labelled in the local language, transparent bins with fill lines, visual recipe cards with no text, and a four-sheet inventory layer with reorder alerts behind them.

Eight to ten defined roles, a cross-training matrix, and a phased rollout running the old and new systems in parallel before switchover.

Vendor qualification

A failed audit, rebuilt cheapest-first

A manufacturer failed a customer's vendor qualification audit. I rebuilt the position by marking every finding twice: what the auditor scored, and what I found myself in the evidence that the auditor had missed. Two of my own findings carried more legal exposure than anything scored.

Five of the audit questions fail you automatically, so fixing everything else perfectly still leaves you non-compliant. The first stage closes those five for a fraction of the total budget, and the expensive building work is sequenced last.

Relocation services

Five systems, none of them talking

Employee onboarding, training and billing all done by hand across Salesforce, Jobber, Appenate, Samsara and Slack. I mapped the workflows end to end and automated them, taking 28% out of operating costs.

Alongside it, a dispatch tool putting driver jobs into Slack as tappable tasks that fed status straight back into Salesforce, presented to the chief executive as the engagement closed.
